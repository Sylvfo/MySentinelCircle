MY SENTINEL CIRCLE — PROJECT PLAN (draft, to be reviewed)
===========================================================

CONCEPT
-------
A mutual safety-net app: each user builds tiered "circles" of trusted people
who watch over them. If a user goes silent, someone can raise an alert —
starting with the closest circle, escalating outward if needed. Works in
reverse too: a third party (e.g. hotel staff, coworker) can alert a person's
circle if something happens to them. Designed to be usable in a real
emergency by people who rarely open the app — SMS-first, minimal friction.

ROLES
-----
- Me          : a registered user; manages their own circles and permissions.
- Companion   : a person "Me" watches over; has an account (a Companion is
                someone else's "Me").
- Sentinel    : a person chosen by "Me" to be part of a circle. Comes in four
                mutually exclusive types (see CIRCLES & PERMISSIONS):
                Lead Sentinel, First Circle Sentinel, Sentinel, Sentinel SMS.
                The first two only exist in the 1st circle and require an
                account; a plain Sentinel (outer circle) also has an
                account, while a Sentinel SMS does not and is reached
                entirely by SMS (accept an invitation, decline, or later opt
                out), identified by phone number. EXCEPTION: a Sentinel of
                the 1st circle MUST have an account, so "Me" can only add to
                the 1st circle a person whose phone already has a registered
                account. Outside the 1st circle an account stays optional (a
                Sentinel may still create one by choice — this upgrades them
                from Sentinel SMS to Sentinel). Conversely, an account-holding
                Sentinel outside the 1st circle who stops logging in falls
                back to Sentinel SMS (inactivity threshold TBD — see OPEN
                POINTS). Must explicitly accept before becoming active —
                "Me" cannot add someone as a Sentinel without their
                validation.
- Organization : (placeholder name, to be finalized) a vetted entity — hotel
                chain, vacation camp, police, school, business, etc. —
                subject to stricter verification than individual users
                (method TBD). Has multiple staff accounts (OrganizationMember)
                who log in on its behalf under internal roles. Once verified,
                logged-in staff can flag a concern about someone with just
                that person's phone number — identified via an authenticated
                API call and restricted to vetted organizations to cut scam 
                risk. Beyond that phone-only alert
                trigger, logging in also opens the door to more rights
                (exact scope TBD — see OPEN POINTS).

ACCOUNTS & BILLING
------------------
- Only "Me" accounts require registration, and they are the paying tier —
  paying is what lets a user be watched over (i.e. be someone else's
  Companion) and manage their own circles. Organizations will also require
  accounts (later, B2B tier). Everyone else — plain Sentinels — needs no
  account: they participate entirely by SMS.
- A Sentinel may still choose to create an account (to use the app UI /
  push), but it is optional and grants no extra obligation. Accepting,
  declining, and opting out of a Sentinel link are all possible by SMS
  reply, from either side of the link, with no login.
- EXCEPTION — 1st circle: its Sentinels MUST have an account (they use the
  app: shared group chat, driving alerts, sending requests to outer circles).
  "Me" can only add someone to the 1st circle if that phone already has a
  registered account; otherwise the invite is refused until they sign up.
  This account requirement applies only to the 1st circle — outer circles
  stay account-free / SMS-only.
- Billing mechanics (plans, what exactly is gated) still TBD — see
  basedb.txt "a reconcilier".

CIRCLES & PERMISSIONS
----------------------
- Each "Me" defines multiple circles (e.g. 1st circle/family, work,
  neighbors), ranked by tier. The 1st circle is the "close circle" and is
  the hub: every other circle only ever interacts with the 1st circle, never
  with each other. It's meant to stay small (advised 4-5 people max, but more
  is allowed), since during an active alert its members share one live group
  chat together (see Alert lifecycle).
- The 1st circle exists by default for every "Me" and MUST contain at least
  one Lead Sentinel (the designated responsible). The Lead Sentinel is the
  person "Me" designates to make the real-life calls and drive alerts —
  deciding what to do, coordinating the other 1st-circle Sentinels, sending
  requests out to the other circles, and closing the alert. There can be
  more than one Lead Sentinel (with an optional succession order — see Alert
  lifecycle); there must always be at least one. Plain First Circle
  Sentinels help the Lead Sentinel(s) but don't drive.
- Role of the other circles (work, sport, neighbors, friends...): their
  Sentinels can ONLY raise the initial alert to the 1st circle (e.g. "haven't
  seen José at work for 5 days"). They never manage or close it. From there
  the 1st circle runs the whole thing: it can send questions to a specific
  outer circle ("is José home?") which its members answer yes/no by SMS or
  by in-app messaging IF that member's link is authorized for it (see
  sentinelType and per-membership messaging settings below). Only the 1st
  circle (or the concerned person themselves) can close an alert.
- Having a first name (prénom) is mandatory for everyone in the system — the
  "Me" account (set at signup) and every Sentinel (set when invited/linked).
  A username is optional (used for invitations — see Circle management).
- Each Sentinel link has exactly one sentinelType, chosen by "Me" per
  Sentinel, which folds together circle tier, driving role, and account
  requirement into four mutually exclusive values:
    - Lead Sentinel        : 1st circle only, has an account, drives alerts.
    - First Circle Sentinel: 1st circle only, has an account, helps but
                              doesn't drive.
    - Sentinel              : any outer circle, has an account — can
                              exchange in-app messages if the link's
                              messaging settings allow it (see below).
    - Sentinel SMS          : any outer circle, no account — reached and
                              acting entirely by SMS, limited to quick
                              predefined replies ("ok vu tel jour", "checker
                              pas vu", etc.); never a two-way conversation,
                              regardless of messaging settings.
  This sentinelType fully replaces the old separate "reference" flag and
  "Sentinel+" type. A Sentinel's messaging reach during an alert is no
  longer a property of a "+" type — it's the combination of (a) having an
  account (Sentinel, not Sentinel SMS) and (b) that link's messaging
  settings being authorized (see below).
- Per-membership messaging settings (only meaningful for account-holding
  Sentinels — Sentinel SMS is always predefined-replies-only): each link
  carries canMessageOwnCircle (can this Sentinel chat with the rest of
  their own circle? default true for the 1st circle) and
  firstCircleMessagingMode (none | individual | group — can this Sentinel
  reach the 1st circle 1:1, in a shared group chat with the 1st circle, or
  not at all? set by "Me" per Sentinel). Outside those authorizations,
  outer-circle Sentinels answer the 1st circle by SMS only.
- The link can be initiated from either side: "Me" can invite someone as a
  Sentinel (by phone number, email, or username), or someone can request to
  become a Sentinel for a given "Me". Either way, the party who didn't
  initiate must validate before the link becomes active — invited Sentinels
  must accept "Me"'s invitation, and "Me" must approve incoming Sentinel
  requests. Only accepted links can receive alerts or count toward a circle.
  "Me" can also decline/block an incoming request (e.g. someone judged
  unsuitable, such as too old to take on the role) — see Circle management
  for the distinction between declining one request and blocking a user
  outright.
- Circles cannot be modified while an alert is active — no adding, removing,
  or editing Sentinel links, for any circle of the concerned "Me". The
  concerned "Me" specifically cannot make ANY changes during their own
  active alert (they're presumed unable to act); their only available
  action is to resolve/close the alert themselves if they're fine, after
  which normal circle editing resumes.
- Organizations bypass circle structure entirely when alerting with just the
  target's phone number, from within the app — the alert always routes to
  the 1st circle first.
- Reserve circle: besides the 1st circle, every "Me" also has exactly one
  system-managed "reserve" circle (circleType RESERVED), created on demand
  and never deletable, same as the 1st circle. It holds accepted Sentinel
  links that exist but aren't currently active in any real circle — a
  Sentinel "Me" pauses without ending the relationship, or the survivors of
  a circle "Me" deletes (e.g. the choir circle closes; its Sentinels move to
  reserve instead of being force-removed). A Sentinel link can NEVER land in
  the reserve circle at creation (invite or request) — it's reachable only
  by a deliberate move afterward (manual pause, or a circle deletion cascade).
  No Lead slot can exist there (Lead is 1st-circle-only, same rule as any
  other outer circle). Once the alert module exists, the reserve circle must
  be excluded from any circle-escalation/broadcast logic — it never receives
  alerts.
- Moving a Sentinel between circles: "Me" can move an already-accepted
  Sentinel to any of their circles (a real one, or the reserve circle) as an
  alternative to removing them outright — nobody has to just disappear from
  "Me"'s circles. Moving into the 1st circle specifically needs the
  Sentinel's consent first (a message, must accept) — same spirit as an
  invite. While they wait, they stay exactly where they currently are; if
  they decline, nothing changes. Moving into any other circle (a BASIC
  circle, or reserve) is immediate, no consent needed — just a notification
  telling them they were moved. Removing a Sentinel from the app/site side
  shows a clear warning first (that relationship really ends), since Move is
  now the non-destructive alternative.

CORE FEATURES (MVP)
--------------------
1. Auth
   - Phone-only signup: a full, permanent account from just a phone number
     + firstName, no email or Google identity required — phone is the
     account's lifelong identifier, verified via OTP, never optional on
     this path.
   - Email+password and Google signup: phone is now OPTIONAL, to let
     someone try the app with minimal friction ("just looking around"
     mode). An account created this way with no phone is marked
     `userType: UNCOMPLETE` (vs `ACCOUNT` once a phone is present) — it can
     browse freely but can't have or be a Sentinel until a phone is added.
     **Enforced**: `inviteSentinel`/`requestToBeSentinel` reject an
     UNCOMPLETE caller (`sentinel.service.ts`'s `assertCompleteAccount`) —
     every other Sentinel-side lookup goes through a phone match, so an
     UNCOMPLETE account can never end up on the other side of a link
     either. "Complete your profile with a phone later" is built:
     `POST /user/phone/request` + `/user/phone/verify` promote
     UNCOMPLETE -> ACCOUNT once verified (frontend: `AddPhonePanel`).
   - Username: optional for phone-only signup. **Mandatory** for
     email+password and Google signup — auto-suggested at signup,
     editable, but can't be skipped or deferred (unlike lastName and phone
     on those two paths, which can be added later). Visible to other users
     as an invitation target — see Circle management. The phone number, by
     contrast, is never shown to other users.
   - On top of the phone, an account may additionally carry an email+
     password and/or a linked Google identity as extra ways to log in or
     recover access — neither is required, and either can be added later
     (e.g. to unlock password reset).
   - Signup/login options: phone + SMS OTP (the baseline, works for every
     account with a phone), plus email + password or Google OAuth — phone
     verification via OTP is mandatory whenever an account has a phone,
     but an `UNCOMPLETE` account (no phone) logs in freely with no OTP step
     at all, nothing to verify.
   - Password reset via email link — only available for accounts that have
     added an email+password credential (n/a for phone-only or Google-only
     accounts, which have no passwordHash to reset).
   - If the phone number changes (lost/replaced device+SIM), a user with
     email+password or Google logs in that way and updates their phone
     number from account settings; the new number is re-verified via OTP
     before use in alerts. A phone-only account with no other credential
     has no recovery path today if the phone is lost — open gap, not yet
     designed.
   - Known accepted gap in `claimOrCreateByPhone` (auth.service.ts): a
     narrow race between the read and the write if two signups for the
     same phone land concurrently. The create-fresh path is protected by
     the DB's `phone` unique constraint; the claim-a-stub path isn't.
     Not hardened for now — revisit later if it matters in practice.

2. Circle management
   - Create circles, invite Sentinels/Companions, set per-member settings
     (sentinelType, and the per-membership messaging settings). Every "Me"
     starts with a default 1st circle that must keep at least one Lead
     Sentinel.
   - A Sentinel link can start from either side: "Me" invites someone (by
     phone, email, or username), or someone requests to become a Sentinel
     for a given "Me". Whichever side didn't initiate must validate before
     the link becomes active.
   - Every new Sentinel is assigned to a circle right away — a Sentinel
     always belongs to exactly one circle from the moment the link exists
     (never the reserve circle — see CIRCLES & PERMISSIONS). For a
     Sentinel-initiated request specifically, that initial circle is just a
     technical placeholder (the 1st circle): since the requester can't see
     "Me"'s private circle list to choose one, "Me" picks the real circle
     when accepting the request, not before. Afterward, "Me" can move an
     accepted link to any of their circles at any time, including into
     reserve (a pause) or out of it.
   - Blocking has two levels: declining a single incoming request (link
     status -> DECLINED, that one request is refused but either side could
     try again later) vs blocking a user outright (link status -> BLOCKED
     on the existing Sentinel link, which — combined with the unique
     sentinel+companion pair constraint — prevents any future request
     between them; only possible after a first link already exists, and a
     BLOCKED link must never be deleted). "Me" can also block an existing
     Sentinel link at any time, ending it.
   - No circle/membership edits are possible while an alert is active for
     that "Me" — see CIRCLES & PERMISSIONS.

3. Alert lifecycle
   - Alert types (AlertType, un seul enum plat — voir schema.prisma):
     BYCOMPANION (le/la compagnon.ne appelle lui/elle-même à l'aide —
     toujours urgent), LITTLEWORRY (petite inquiétude, pas d'urgence,
     pas de recherche active), MISSING (on ne retrouve plus le/la
     compagnon.ne — recherche active), INCIDENT (le/la compagnon.ne a
     eu un problème), EMERGENCY, NOCONTACT (pas de nouvelles depuis un
     moment, check-in manqué, sans être MISSING), UNKNOWN (defaut avant
     classification).
     Pour INCIDENT: a Sentinel or Organization reports that something
     already happened, and leaves their own callback phone number so
     the Lead Sentinel can call them directly (in-app call/discussion,
     or an outside call once contact has been made — see DATA MODEL:
     Alert.reporterPhone). The Companion later sees, in their history,
     WHO called WHOM and when for that alert, but not the call content.
   Either way, a reason message (free text describing why the alert is
   being raised) is required before it dispatches.
   - A verified Organization raises an alert from within the app: a logged-in
     OrganizationMember, identified via an authenticated API call rather
     than anonymous SMS, submits the target's phone number plus a reason ->
     1st circle notified immediately -> an in-app chat opens between the
     Organization and the 1st circle for follow-up (see DATA MODEL:
     Conversation). No prior link between the Organization and the target
     person is required (e.g. a hotel reporting a guest they just found
     injured). Anonymous, non-organization phone-only alerts are no longer
     allowed — judged too easy to abuse for scams.
     MVP scope: ship a small, minimal slice of this first (raise + notify
     1st circle + open chat) — the fuller Organization verification/
     dashboard work (see OPEN POINTS) can follow later; Organizations stay
     in the model even at this stage since they're a potential paying
     customer segment (B2B), not just a nice-to-have.
   - Organization flow: look up the target user by phone number -> notify
     the 1st circle -> open a dedicated Organization<->1st circle chat for
     that alert.
   - The "Me" the alert concerns is also contacted directly (SMS/push) as
     soon as the alert is raised — not just their circle. "Me" can take
     control of the alert at any point (e.g. mark themselves safe / resolve
     it directly), overriding whatever the circle is doing; the circle is
     then notified the alert was closed by "Me".
   - Only 1st circle members or "Me" can close an alert — other circles can
     only answer the 1st circle's requests, never manage or close the alert.
     When the person can't be reached, the 1st circle involves the relevant
     outer circle(s) by sending them a request (they reply yes/no); the
     1st circle stays in charge throughout.
   - Once an alert is active, all 1st circle members share one live group
     chat ("1st circle chat"), scoped to that alert and closed automatically
     when it resolves — that's what keeps the 1st circle intentionally
     small. Sentinels outside the 1st circle can't join this chat. An
     account-holding outer-circle Sentinel whose link is authorized for it
     (firstCircleMessagingMode = individual or group — see CIRCLES &
     PERMISSIONS) can request, during the alert, to open a chat with the
     1st circle — each requesting Sentinel gets their own isolated chat
     with the 1st circle, not one chat shared across every requester.
   - Lead handoff: if no Lead Sentinel responds/connects within the default
     wait window (configurable, exact default TBD — see OPEN POINTS),
     leadership passes to the next Lead Sentinel in the 1st circle's
     succession order (optional — a 1st circle isn't required to have more
     than one Lead Sentinel). This is recorded per-alert as "who actually
     took the lead," independently of who's the default-designated Lead
     Sentinel(s). Meanwhile, other 1st-circle members aren't blocked from
     acting (e.g. starting to look) just because no lead has responded yet
     — the lead's role is specifically to make real-life decisions once the
     Companion is found and something needs deciding.
   - Notifications go out via SMS (and push, for app users); replies parsed
     automatically (predefined keywords for Sentinel SMS / account-less
     replies, free text/media for account-holding Sentinels once
     authorized). Availability reply scale (used when asking a Sentinel to
     take/join a search):
       - 0  : pas disponible pour l'instant
       - 1  : disponible dans 2-3h
       - 2  : disponible dans 4-6h
       - 3  : disponible dans 12h
       - 4  : disponible dans 24h
       - 5  : disponible dans 2 jours
       - 6  : disponible dans 1 semaine
       - 10 : ne veut plus faire partie du cercle (triggers opt-out)
     A reply can arrive by SMS (parsed then sent back for confirmation) or
     by logging into the app. A thank-you/acknowledgement message is sent
     back after each reply. When looking for the Companion, prefer asking
     everyone in a solicited circle rather than a single member (more
     corroborating data), but stop collecting further "seen" sightings for
     the same day once 3 people have confirmed one. At alert close, every
     Sentinel who participated receives a closing message — no exceptions.

4. Messagerie Me <-> Sentinels
   - Outside of any active alert, "Me" can always reach any of their
     Sentinels 1:1 — in-app if they hold an account, by SMS if they're
     Sentinel SMS — regardless of sentinelType. This everyday messaging
     isn't gated by the per-membership messaging settings (those only
     govern a Sentinel's reach towards the 1st circle during an alert).
   - This messaging is always Me<->Sentinel (1:1). Sentinels never message
     each other directly outside of an active alert. During an active
     alert, several scoped group chats can exist instead (see Alert
     lifecycle): the 1st circle's own group chat between its members; on
     request, a separate chat between one authorized account-holding
     Sentinel and the 1st circle; and, for Organization-raised alerts, a
     chat between the Organization and the 1st circle. All are scoped to
     that specific alert, close automatically when it resolves, and don't
     exist outside of it.

5. History / audit trail
   - Per user: full log of alerts raised, who was contacted, who responded
     (and how), and the underlying message history — including, for
     Incident-type alerts, who called whom and when (Sentinel/Organization
     reporter <-> Lead Sentinel) and what was communicated. "Me" can see
     the full history of every conversation involving their own alerts:
     between Sentinels, and between circles.

DATA MODEL (sketch)
--------------------
- User              : account holder (firstName, username?, email,
                       passwordHash, phone), dual auth. firstName and
                       phone are required for every User row — a
                       Sentinel without an account is simply a User row
                       with only firstName+phone filled in (no separate
                       Contact model; matches schema.prisma).
- Circle             : belongs to a User, has a label; exactly one circle
                       per owner is the 1st circle (isPrimary).
- CircleMembership   : links a User to a Circle, with sentinelType
                       (lead_sentinel | first_circle_sentinel | sentinel |
                       sentinel_sms — lead_sentinel and first_circle_
                       sentinel only valid when circle.isPrimary), an
                       optional leadSuccessionOrder (int, only meaningful
                       for lead_sentinel rows in the same 1st circle — who
                       takes over if the previous lead is unavailable),
                       canMessageOwnCircle (bool, default true) and
                       firstCircleMessagingMode (none | individual | group)
                       governing this Sentinel's reach during an alert,
                       status (pending | accepted | declined | blocked) and
                       initiatedBy (me | sentinel) — tracks whether "Me"
                       sent the invitation or the Sentinel requested the
                       link, since the other party is the one who must
                       validate it.
- Organization       : a vetted entity (hotel, school, police, business...)
                       that can raise phone-only alerts once verified;
                       verification status (pending/verified/rejected) and
                       method TBD.
- OrganizationMember : links a User (staff) to an Organization, with an
                       internal role (e.g. admin, staff) governing what they
                       can do when acting on the Organization's behalf.
- Alert              : raised for a target User, with type: one of
                       BYCOMPANION | LITTLEWORRY | MISSING | INCIDENT |
                       EMERGENCY | NOCONTACT | UNKNOWN (see
                       schema.prisma AlertType), status
                       (active/escalated/resolved), origin
                       (Sentinel via app | Sentinel via SMS | Organization
                       via app), a required reason (free text, why the alert
                       is being raised — collected up front at creation time
                       for every origin), resolvedBy (target user themselves
                       vs a circle contact). For type=incident: reporterPhone
                       (the reporting Sentinel/Organization's callback
                       number for the Lead Sentinel to call). Also tracks
                       currentLeadUserId (who actually took the lead for
                       THIS alert — may differ from the 1st circle's
                       default-designated lead_sentinel(s) if unavailable,
                       see CIRCLES & PERMISSIONS). CompagnonStatut and
                       1erCercleStatut (finer-grained progress states) —
                       fields TBD, see OPEN POINTS.
- AlertNotification  : one row per contact notified for a given alert,
                       including the target User themselves (they're
                       always notified directly, not only their circle);
                       delivery status, availabilityCode (the 0-6/10 SMS
                       reply scale — see Alert lifecycle), respondedAt.
- Conversation       : a generic thread, reused for all 3 messaging
                       contexts instead of a separate AlertChat model.
                       Attached via an optional unique conversationId on
                       whichever parent it belongs to: LinkSentinels
                       (Me<->Sentinel 1:1, outside any alert),
                       CircleAlert (the 1st circle's own group chat
                       during an alert — only meaningful when
                       isPrimary), or LinkSentinelAlert (isolated chat
                       between one requesting Sentinel and the 1st
                       circle during an alert; requires that link's
                       firstCircleMessagingMode != none). Created lazily
                       — only when the first message is sent. No `type`
                       field needed: which parent holds the
                       conversationId already tells you the kind.
                       Organization<->1st circle chat deferred until
                       Organization is implemented (see OPEN POINTS).
- Message            : id, body, type (see schema.prisma MessageType:
                       text | image | system), senderId (always a
                       User — the sender's identity doesn't need to
                       survive as a snapshot, unlike Alert.alertBy/
                       leadBy/closedBy, since Users are only ever
                       soft-deleted), conversationId (required, onDelete
                       Cascade — deleting a Conversation deletes its
                       Messages). During an active alert, Sentinel SMS
                       replies are limited to the fixed availability
                       code scale and aren't stored as Messages. Outside
                       of an alert, any account-holding Sentinel can
                       message "Me" freely.

BACKEND MODULES (NestJS, sketch)
----------------------------------
- Auth        : login/signup itself — phone+OTP, email+password, Google
                OAuth; issues sessions/JWT. Kept separate from User so auth
                strategies can evolve independently of profile data.
- User        : create/modify account info; read own data (incl. alert
                history); accept/decline incoming Sentinel invitations.
- Sentinel    : register Sentinels (either direction — invite or request),
                define circles, set per-Sentinel settings (sentinelType, and
                the reference/responsible flag for 1st-circle members).
                Keeps the default 1st circle with >=1 reference Sentinel.
- Alert       : send alerts (app + SMS), respond to an alert, notify the
                relevant circles, notify everyone once an alert is closed.
                Enforces that only 1st circle members or "Me" can close it.
- Organization : authenticate the OrganizationMember, look up a target user
                by phone number, raise the alert (with reason), notify the
                1st circle, and open the Organization<->1st circle
                Conversation for that alert.
- Messaging   : everyday Me<->Sentinel conversations outside of any active
                alert (open to any Sentinel, base or +); also backs the
                in-alert Conversations used by Alert (the 1st circle's own
                chat, one isolated chat per requesting Sentinel+, and the
                Organization chat for Organization-raised alerts), but the
                two contexts enforce different rules (free-form always
                here vs Sentinel-type-gated and request-based during an
                alert).

TECH STACK (confirmed)
------------------------
- Backend      : NestJS + TypeScript
- Frontend web : React + TypeScript
- Mobile       : React Native
- ORM / DB     : Prisma + PostgreSQL, hosted on Neon (cloud, no local install)
